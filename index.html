Before you start
Get-ready lead-in

The lead-in counts you in before timing starts, giving you time to set the water properly cold and step in — the ring empties as it counts, with soft beeps for the final 3 and a “GO”. Pick 10, 20 or 30 seconds to suit how long your tap takes to run cold. Tap the dial to skip it any time.

Play over the ring switch

Minute beeps count the minutes out loud: one beep at 1:00, two at 2:00, three at 3:00 and so on. Count them and you know exactly how long you've been in — eyes shut, back to the phone, no glasses. They ring on a warmer, rounder note than the sharp 3-2-1 beeps before the finish, so the two can't be confused, and they stop at your goal. Keep chiming repeats the goal chime every 20 seconds through the bonus count-up, so the sound keeps telling you what the flashing green screen is telling you.

Play over the ring switch routes every cue through the music channel instead of the ringer, so you still hear them with the phone on silent — which is the only way they work for most mornings. The trade-off: while a session is running this can pause music or a podcast you had playing. Turn it off if you'd rather keep your audio and watch the screen instead.

All cues are pitched and layered to carry over running water. Preview them with the test timer above.
While it runs
Hold the button to stop

With this on, ■ STOP has to be held for about a second — a white bar sweeps across the button as you hold, and letting go early cancels it. Wet hands and a phone at chest height make a single accidental tap far too easy, and that tap used to end your shower.

Whatever happens, the app stops itself after 15 minutes of bonus time and saves the session. If you step out and forget the phone, it can no longer count up all morning holding the screen awake and then log a nonsense time.

How this works
Getting started ›

Pick a goal with the 3 / 6 / 9-minute presets, or nudge it ±1 minute with the − / + buttons.

Tap ▶ START and a lead-in counts you in, giving you time to set the water to cold and step in before timing begins. Just the number counts down, nice and big. Set it to 10, 20 or 30 seconds under Settings → Before you start, tap the dial to skip it, or switch it off entirely.

Let the ring count you down. Hold the big ■ STOP button for about a second to end and save the session — a white bar sweeps across it as you hold, and letting go early cancels. Holding rather than tapping means a wet hand brushing the phone can't end your shower; if you'd rather have a plain tap, turn it off under Settings → While it runs.

The button is a signal in itself: red while you're short of your goal, green the moment you've made it, and gold if you go past your longest-ever shower.

Under the title, one line tells you how many days running you've kept it up and how long your last shower was. It takes the place of the “set your goal” tagline rather than adding a line to the screen — so the START button keeps its distance from the presets. Before your first shower the tagline shows instead, and the line disappears with everything else once a session starts.

Reading it with no glasses ›

You're not wearing glasses in the shower, so the moment a session starts the screen strips itself back: the title, tagline, version line and every small caption disappear until you're out.

What's left is only what still reads blurred, from a metre away, through running water:

1 · The number. It resizes itself to be as large as will fit inside the ring — so 10 during the lead-in fills the dial, and +10:00 in bonus time still fits. It glows in the colour of the phase you're in.

2 · The three bars under the dial — one per third of your goal — filling left to right in blue → yellow → green. Even if you can't read a single digit, how full the bars are tells you how far along you are.

3 · The colour of the whole screen, which takes on a wash of the phase colour, then turns fully green the instant you hit your goal.

4 · One giant button, which is also a signal in itself: red means you haven't reached your goal yet, green means you have, gold means you're past your longest-ever shower. Nothing else is tappable, so you can't miss it or hit the wrong thing with wet hands — and it has to be held, so a stray splash or a knuckle can't end the session either.

Gold beats green. The moment you go past your record the entire screen — wash, ring, digits and button — switches from green to gold, with a brighter flourish. You don't have to wait until you've stopped to find out you've done it.

No symbols to decipher. A little emoji is no easier to make out than a word when everything's blurred, so the shower screen doesn't use any — the get-in 🚿, the goal-reached ✓ and the finish-screen trophy have all been removed. Only ▶ and ■ remain, sitting inside the one huge button where there's nothing to confuse them with.

And the screen after you stop is down to two things: one big number (how long you lasted) and one big button. How it went is the colour of the whole screen — green if you made your goal, amber if you came close (within a tenth of it), icy blue if you stopped well short, gold for a personal best — so there's genuinely nothing to read while you're dripping.

The amber matters: stopping at 5:50 on a 6-minute goal is not the same story as stopping at 30 seconds, and it no longer looks like it.

Your goal, how far over or under you were, and everything else moved to Stats, for when your glasses are back on. Press DONE to get out of the finish screen, then open Stats with the chart button at the top of the home screen.

The countdown, colours & finish ›

The timer runs through three colour-coded phases across equal thirds of your goal: blue → yellow → green. The ring, the digits, the three bars under the dial and a wash across the whole screen all carry that colour at once, so a blurry glance tells you how far you've come.

The three bars under the dial are one per third. Each fills left to right as you move through it — a progress reading that needs no numbers at all.

The minutes are counted out loud. One beep when a minute has gone, two beeps at two minutes, three at three, and so on — so you always know how long you've been in without opening your eyes or turning round. The beeps are long and unhurried, on a warmer note than the short, sharp 3-2-1 beeps just before the finish, so you can't mistake one for the other. They stop the moment you reach your goal, where the goal chime takes over. Turn minute beeps off under Settings → Sounds if you prefer silence.

Every cue is built to survive running water. A single pure tone is the easiest thing in the world for a shower to swallow, so each beep and chime is a stack of several tones an octave and a twelfth apart, pitched where the ear hears best. They also play over the ring switch, so you still hear them with the phone on silent — see Settings → Sounds, where you can turn that off if you'd rather it never paused your music.

You'll hear soft 3-2-1 beeps just before the finish, then a chime and three big green flashes the instant you hit your goal.

The flashing then keeps going — settling into a slower, gentler green pulse that carries on until you press ■ STOP. So if you happened to be facing away at the exact second you hit your goal, the screen is still waving at you when you next glance over; you can't miss it any more.

At the same time the whole background turns green and stays green for the rest of the session, and the big button turns from red to green — three separate ways of saying the same thing, none of which need your glasses.

And the chime keeps going too. A softer version of it repeats every 20 seconds through the bonus count-up, so the sound is saying the same thing the pulsing green screen is saying, for as long as you're in there. Turn it off under Settings → Sounds.

After the goal it flips to a bonus count-up, so you can push past your target and bank extra seconds. Go past your longest-ever shower and the whole screen turns gold at that exact moment, with a brighter flourish — so the bonus time has something to aim at rather than just climbing. The finish screen then keeps the gold, with a short “New best” badge, and the session is flagged with a 🏅 in your history.

It stops itself eventually. After 15 minutes of bonus time the app ends the session and saves it. If you step out and leave the phone running, it can no longer count up all morning holding the screen awake and then record a nonsense time.

Profiles & stats ›

Add a profile per person from the name chip at the top left. Each keeps their own goal and their own history.

The stats screen shows sessions, total time, your longest shower, goal-hit rate, day-streak and average — plus recent-session, calendar and weekly charts. Record-setting sessions are marked with a 🏅 in the recent list.

Tap Adjust under “Recent sessions” to fix your history: add a shower you forgot to time (set its date, length and goal) or delete one that was logged by mistake.

The list starts with your 20 most recent showers and a Show older button underneath brings back 20 more each time, all the way to your first. Older sessions can be adjusted and deleted just like recent ones.

Backup & test timer ›

At the very bottom of Settings, under Your data & history → Backup & restore, you can export a backup file, copy it to the clipboard, or import / restore one — handy for keeping your history safe or moving it to a new phone. A restore shows its work: it reports exactly how many profiles and sessions came back, and warns you if anything didn't make it (your backup file stays untouched, so you can just try again).

Once you've built up some history, a slim backup reminder appears on the home screen if it's been over a month since your last backup. Tap Back up to jump to your data options, or ✕ to dismiss it for a week. Any export, copy or restore resets the reminder.

The 30-second test timer (also in Settings) runs a quick countdown so you can preview the beeps, chime and green finish without a full shower. Test runs are never saved to your stats.

If the app closes mid-shower — the phone reboots, iPhone reclaims memory, or it gets swiped away by mistake — the session is no longer lost. While a session runs the app quietly notes it down every couple of seconds, and next time you open it you're shown how far you'd got and asked whether to keep it or discard it. At worst you lose the last second or two, instead of the whole shower.

Offline & Shortcuts ›

Add to Home Screen from Safari's Share menu and it runs full-screen and fully offline, like a real app.

It keeps the screen awake during a session. You can also auto-start it from an Apple Shortcut — open it with ?goal=6 to preset minutes and ?start to begin immediately.

“Tap for sound”. When a Shortcut opens the app, no finger has touched the page — and iPhone blocks a web app from making any sound until it has been touched. That used to leave the entire shower silent: no lead-in beeps, no minute beeps, no goal chime. So a Shortcut launch now shows a Tap for sound prompt over the dial. One tap anywhere on it wakes the sound for the whole session, and unlike tapping the dial itself, it will not skip your lead-in. The timer starts immediately either way — the tap only concerns the sound — and the ■ STOP button stays reachable underneath it.
